Why this matters

Discovery today is slow and manual. As packs grow and fragment, people spend their time reconciling documents instead of finding the answer, and the findings they write are hard to check.

Can AI, with the right prompts, turn a scattered evidence pack into a discovery report a sponsor can trust?

This hub showcases a repeatable discovery workflow and the prompts behind it, run end to end on one fictional programme.

Today

Evidence arrives scattered across spreadsheets, documents, emails and data extracts. Sources contradict each other. Findings get written from memory and confidence, not from a traceable trail, and the whole thing is redone by hand on the next programme.

What this hub shows

A fixed workflow of structured prompts that turns a messy pack into sourced, checkable analysis, then into the report and deck a programme needs. The prompt library holds every prompt, ready to reuse.

The business view

Apex Racing believes its legacy race-strategy platform, RaceView, may be contributing to avoidable race performance loss. Leadership needs to decide whether RaceView should be modernised, integrated around or replaced.

"My current view is that pit-stop inconsistency is our biggest avoidable source of race loss, and RaceView is likely contributing because the platform is showing its age."

Elena Ward, Team Principal and sponsor. Email, 18 June.

The discovery challenge

The team has collected a lot of material, but it doesn't point to a single cause. Documents, interviews, incidents, operational data and technical records each tell part of the story, and sometimes contradict one another.

Two things need to happen before system changes go ahead: the CFO wants a costed option before any replacement commitment, and Technical Governance needs parallel running, rollback evidence and a migration rehearsal for any core replacement.

Zero-shot output

Slow pit stops are the visible problem. They might not be the real cause. In controlled practice, stops average 2.29 seconds. During races, that rises to 3.23 seconds. We need to understand what's driving the gap.

A conclusion stated as fact, with no document named. It also goes on to list "emerging implications", which is solution design the brief didn't ask for.

Structured output

The apparent pit-stop problem includes both upstream timing and genuine execution defects. The sample is small, so averages alone cannot isolate causality.

Why the sponsor can trust it

  • Every finding traced to a named source document
  • Confirmed evidence kept apart from inference
  • Confidence rated, with the reason
  • Contradictions surfaced, not smoothed over
  • Uncertainty made explicit: gaps listed, not filled in
  • A human review before anything reaches the sponsor

What the evidence doesn't support yet

Saying so is part of being trustworthy. On current evidence, discovery would not recommend:

  • Committing to a RaceView core replacement
  • Approving two more strategy analysts
  • Targeting pit-crew execution from the headline stop average
  • Reporting any decision-latency trend
